Draft written 2026-11-08, updated 2026-11-11. Numbers come from `analysis/fullscale/tables.md`, generated by `trace-pipeline/tools/fullscale_report.py` from `cluster-data/results/fullscale/`, or from the dated `context.md` sections cited in brackets (those numbers are read from raw files in `cluster-data/results/fullscale/` by the agent that ran them; the generated table covers only the batched-decode section). Section names below refer to that file. Figures: `analysis/fullscale/fig_reads_per_token.png`, `fig_llc_sweep.png`, `fig_region_time.png`, `fig_batched.png`. This file covers the full-scale DynamoRIO route; `ANALYSIS.md` covers the gem5-era runs and is unchanged.

## 1. Method

- Models: Qwen2.5-0.5B (Q4_0), Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (Q4_K_M), Qwen3-30B-A3B (Q4_K_M, MoE with 128 experts, 8 active). llama.cpp native build with AVX-512 and 4 threads, run on node01, node03 or node06.
- Capture: DynamoRIO attaches to one decode step (a "burst") and detaches. Step 1 for short prompts (12 tokens), step 4 after an 8k prompt, step 16 after a 32k prompt. Each burst is replayed through a cold drcachesim hierarchy (22 MiB, 11-way LLC), and the LL misses plus prefetch misses become the DRAM read stream.
- Validation: per-token DRAM reads for Qwen 0.5B match the gem5 captures within 0.7% (`context.md`). Every traced run produced the same text as its untraced run (recorded in `context.md`; the report does not recheck this). The traced step's instruction count and native step time are in "Runs and validation".
- Replay: Ramulator 2.1 on the read stream, saturated, no timestamps, DDR4 (1 channel) and DDR5 (16 sub-channels), mappers RoBaRaCoCh, MOP4CLXOR, ChRaBaRoCo, policies Open and ClosedCAP. An offline in-order row-hit model covers three physical placements: VA-order packed, 4 KiB shuffled, 2 MiB (THP) shuffled.
- Attribution: separate `drattr` runs dump tensor addresses, so each DRAM read is assigned to dense weights, expert weights, KV cache, activations or unattributed.
- Region study: the attributed stream is remapped by identity, bit reversal, a global XOR and a region-aware scheme, then replayed (12M-read windows).

## 2. Findings so far

**Reads per token and the weights sweep.** At short context the model streams its weights once per token: 4.40 GB for Qwen 7B (93.9% of the 4.68 GB file) and 0.35 GB for 0.5B (81.9%). Prefetch changes the read count by under 0.3% ("DRAM reads per token"). Each compute thread's stream is sequential: +1 line is 63% of reads and 31 to 32% fall within 16 lines, with 0.4 to 1.1% far jumps ("Stride"). The merged stream looks random (60% "beyond" for 7B) only because four such streams interleave.

**Row hits.** In-order offline hit is 95.5 to 96.9% for RoRaBaCoCh and for XOR-bank at short context in all three models, and 93 to 96% for shuffled 4 KiB pages. Ramulator with RoBaRaCoCh and Open page gives 97.6 to 98.5% hits on DDR4 and DDR5. ClosedCAP drops hits to about 78% and costs 15 to 33% more time on DDR4. A column-high mapper (RoCoRaBaCh) gets 1.5 to 20%.

**DDR4 vs DDR5.** On one DDR4 channel the mapping matters for time: MOP4CLXOR is 16 to 25% faster than RoBaRaCoCh/Open despite only 38 to 63% hits, because it spreads the stream over more banks (about 17.9 GB/s against 14 to 16 GB/s). On the 16-sub-channel DDR5 it is within 2% (0.98 to 1.00), so mapping barely matters for weight sweeps. ChRaBaRoCo is worst everywhere (1.2 to 1.4x on DDR4, about 2x on DDR5).

**KV amplification at 32k: 1.6 to 1.9 times the expected traffic, and why.** Qwen 7B at 31,001 tokens is expected to read 6.18 GB per token (4.40 GB weights + 1.78 GB KV, one pass each). Three separate traces of the same prompt, with the same 22 MiB 11-way LLC, measured 9.77, 10.31 and 11.49 GB (1.58x, 1.67x, 1.86x) [`context.md`, "The 9.8 / 10.3 / 11.5 GB discrepancy at 32k explained"]. The spread comes from thread scheduling: ggml hands out chunks dynamically, so which thread reads which K/V block, and when, differs between runs, and drcachesim interleaves the four traces in the shared LLC. When one layer's KV (63.5 MB) is about 3x the LLC, whether the seven query heads that share a KV head find their K/V block still cached depends strongly on that order. Where nothing thrashes the runs agree: 68.69M vs 68.68M lines at short context, flat at 8k. We therefore quote **1.6 to 1.9x** and not a single value. The attribution run (the 1.86x one) puts KV at 7.06 GB, 61.5% of reads, about 4x its 1.78 GB size; treat that 4x as the high end of the range.

The LLC sweep confirms the capacity explanation [`context.md`, "LL sweep at 32k"; also "LL sweep" table in `tables.md`]: reads of 161.3M lines (10.32 GB) at 22 MiB fall to 6.99 GB at 32 MiB, 6.21 GB at 64 MiB and 6.16 GB at 128 MiB, equal to the expected 6.18 GB once the LLC holds one layer's K+V (63.5 MB). At 8k the sweep is flat (75.6M to 75.0M lines). Qwen 0.5B at 31,001 tokens (746 MB measured against 732 MB expected, ratio 1.02) and Qwen3-30B-A3B at 7,356 tokens (2.70 GB against 2.66 GB, ratio 1.01) do not amplify, since their per-layer KV (12 MB and 15 MB) fits the 22 MiB LLC. Amplification needs per-layer KV above the LLC.

**Placement.** THP (2 MiB) placement hurts KV row hits: for the 7B at 32k the KV class drops from 92.3% (packed) to 72.4% under RoRaBaCoCh, and the 0.5B KV class to 52.4%. An XOR-bank mapping recovers it to 87.0% (7B) and 96.3% (0.5B). Dense and expert weights are unaffected (96 to 97%).

**MoE.** Qwen3-30B-A3B reads 1.94 GB per token, 10.5% of its 18.56 GB file. Attribution splits this into expert weights 56.5% and dense weights 42.3%. Every expert tensor (144 of them) has exactly 8 of 128 experts touched. At 8k context the total rises to 2.70 GB (14.5%), consistent with the expected 2.67 GB.

*Across tokens* [`context.md`, "MoE across tokens", jobs 119402, 119403; decode steps 1 to 4]: per-token reads are the same as for a single-token run in both models (30B: 30.4M lines against 30.3M; 7B 68.7M against 68.7M), so the LLC keeps nothing between tokens. For the dense 7B, four tokens touch the same bytes as one (footprint 4.38 GB against 4.40 GB per token). For the 30B, four tokens touch 3.02 GB of 4 KiB pages, 1.55x one token's 1.94 GB: consecutive tokens choose strongly overlapping experts (the union is 39% of four times the reads; 25% would be full sharing and 100% disjoint). About 61% of the four-token reads re-fetch data an earlier token already fetched, but the 3 GB union is far beyond an LLC.

*Expert-aware address mapping at B=1* [`context.md`, "status, 2026-11-11", job 120933; `cluster-data/results/fullscale/moe/Q30B_short/`]: identity, global XOR, region, expert_hash, expert_lin and region_expert are all within about 1% of each other (DDR4 Open 30.3 to 30.6 Mcycles; DDR5 3.4), packed and THP placements. This is a negative result: with 8 experts read as whole contiguous slices the default mapping already streams them at about 0.96 row hit, so there is nothing for an expert-aware mapping to fix at batch 1. Whether it helps on the batched streams is pending.

**Literature mappings and the region-aware scheme** [`context.md`, "dev agent round 2", full table in `analysis/proposal_results.md`; `cluster-data/results/fullscale/region/`]. Bit reversal hurts packed placements: 1.16 to 1.44x on the 7B at 8k, 1.13 to 1.28x for the 30B, up to 2.4x with ClosedCAP on the 32k window; it helps only where the default is bad (THP). A global XOR is neutral (0.98 to 1.01) on packed placements and helps only where the KV is on THP pages: cycles relative to the default mapping 0.83 (DDR5, Open) and 0.71 (DDR5, ClosedCAP) on the 32k window, 0.93 and 0.86 at 8k, 0.92 on DDR4 (32k). With THP at 32k on DDR5, bandwidth rises from 114.7 to 138.0 GB/s, row hit 0.85 to 0.95.

**Region-aware does not beat a global XOR hash.** The region scheme tags each 2 MiB granule as weights (identity), KV or other (own bank-index XOR). In every condition tested it lies within 0.5% of the better of global XOR (ahead by under 1% in some, behind by up to 2.5% in packed 8k DDR5 ClosedCAP). The gain over the default comes from hashing the KV and activation granules, and the global XOR obtains it too while leaving weights essentially untouched. For the 30B at short context (KV 0.2% of reads) nothing helps: all schemes agree within 0.5%, and DDR5 sustains about 271 of 307 GB/s with the default. The data supports "an XOR bank hash on KV and activation pages" as the useful mechanism, not a per-class choice of mapping. Only THP placement matters; with VA-order packed placement the default is within 1% of every alternative at every context length.

**Batched decode** (table "Batched decode" in `tables.md`, figure `fig_batched.png`; raw files `cluster-data/results/fullscale/drbatch/<model>_B<B>_P512t4/`; B sequences with 512-token prompts decode one step together, 4 threads, 22 MiB LLC; tokens of traced and untraced runs identical in all six runs [`context.md`, "status, 2026-11-11"]):

| Qwen 7B | 1 / 4 / 16 | 4.43 / 4.56 / 5.10 | 4.43 / 1.14 / 0.32 | 1.00 / 1.03 / 1.15 | 174 / 283 / 855 ms | - |
| Qwen3-30B-A3B | 1 / 4 / 16 | 1.99 / 4.29 / 9.85 | 1.99 / 1.07 / 0.62 | 1.00 / 2.15 / 4.94 | 104 / 291 / 779 ms | 8.0 / 23.2 / 57.2 |

- Dense: weights are read once per step whatever B is, so DRAM traffic per sequence falls 14x at B=16 (KV and activations add 15%).
- MoE: the step reads the union of the sequences' experts. Touched experts grow 8, 23.2, 57.2 per tensor, against 8, 32, 128 if sequences chose disjoint experts, so the union is 72% and 45% of the disjoint bound at B=4 and 16. Sequences share experts, but far less than the weights of a dense model: at B=16 the 30B reads 0.62 GB per sequence against 0.32 GB for the 7B, though its active weights are less than half the 7B's.
- XOR bank hash (MOP4CLXOR over RoBaRaCoCh, Open page) on DDR5 with 16 sub-channels stays at 0.95 to 0.99 of the default for all B. On one DDR4 channel the MoE gain grows with B (ratio 0.85, 0.84, 0.81) and the dense gain shrinks (0.88, 0.91, 0.97) [`context.md`, same section]. These are saturated open-loop ratios.
- The 30B B=4 native step is 291 ms here and 276 ms in the earlier partial note; the table uses the later measurement.

## 3. What the results cannot support

- **Open loop, saturated.** Ramulator ignores timestamps and issues as fast as the controller accepts. Throughputs and relative times are bandwidth-bound upper bounds, not token latencies, and the MOP-over-RoBaRaCoCh gain on DDR4 depends on that saturation.
- **No writes.** The replayed stream is reads only. KV appends, activations written back and writeback-induced bus turnarounds are missing.
- **Few tokens per configuration.** Most rows are a single decode step. Four consecutive tokens were measured only for the 7B and 30B at short context (reads identical per token).
- **32k absolute numbers are a range.** Three traces of the same 7B 32k token give 9.77, 10.31 and 11.49 GB because of thread scheduling under LLC thrashing (above). The 4x KV amplification and the 61.5% KV share come from the 11.49 GB run and are the high end.
- **Same-token optimism.** The XOR matrices of the region study are searched on the token they are evaluated on (in sample). A held-out token (different position, so different KV addresses) is not yet tested. THP frames are random over 8 GiB, an aged-allocator assumption.
- **Cold caches per window**, a single LLC configuration for the headline numbers, and the region comparison uses 12M-read windows (for 32k the "early" window), not the whole token.
- **Unexplained:** the 7B at 32k on 16-channel DDR5 reaches 142 GB/s against about 270 GB/s for short context, which suggests channel or bank imbalance in the KV stream. Not yet investigated. (The THP row-conflict explanation in the region study is a candidate.)
